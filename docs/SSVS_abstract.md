Comparisons of Mendelian genetic diseases between species represents a valuable resource to increase our understanding of their mechanisms and development.
Recent advances in the field of phenomics have enabled creation of robust semantic frameworks (ontologies) which facilitate translation of our understanding of genetic diseases between species.
This allows the scope of model organisms to be broadened beyond traditional model species like mice and zebrafish, and allows for reciprocal knowledge transfer between human and veterinary medicine.
This presentation explores the merits and current methods of knowledge transfer of genetic diseases between species, with a particular focus on the proposed role of non-traditional model organisms including domestic animals and livestock.
